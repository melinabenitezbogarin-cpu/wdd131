Know

What happens in our minds when we are sleeping?

Since I was little, the capabilities of our minds have always called my attention. How is it possible for our brain to craft intricate, movie-like scenarios every night? Driven by these questions, I began exploring the science of sleep, dream architecture, and how different brain structures work together during rest.

How does our brain process dreams and memories?

Hippocampus

The hippocampus plays a central role in memory consolidation during sleep. While you rest, it replays events from your waking day and transfers temporary short-term memories into the cerebral cortex for long-term storage. During REM sleep, the hippocampus activates strongly to weave recent experiences, emotions, and past memories together into vivid dream scenarios.

Amygdala

As the brain's primary emotional center, the amygdala becomes highly active during REM sleep, often even more active than when we are awake. It processes deep emotional experiences, fears, and stress, which explains why dreams can feel so intense or anxiety-inducing. By re-activating emotional memories in a safe, sleeping state, the amygdala helps process unresolved feelings and balance our emotional health.

Cerebral Cortex

The cerebral cortex—specifically sensory and visual areas—creates the vivid imagery, sounds, and narrative movements we experience while dreaming. Interestingly, while visual areas are hyperactive, the prefrontal cortex (responsible for logic and critical thinking) drops its activity. This temporary deactivation is precisely why strange, illogical dream scenarios feel completely normal until we wake up.

Habits for Better Sleep

Why is it important to study sleep science?

Understanding sleep science is crucial for mental health because quality sleep acts as a natural emotional reset button. During your daily life, the brain regulates neurotransmitters, reduces cortisol levels, and archives daily stress. Prioritizing healthy sleep hygiene significantly decreases the risk of chronic anxiety, depression, and mood disorders, allowing us to maintain psychological resilience and daily cognitive focus.

From a physical standpoint, deep sleep (NREM) is essential for cellular repair, tissue growth, and immune system strengthening. While we dream, the brain also activates the glymphatic system to clear out neurotoxic waste buildup that accumulates during waking hours. Studying and implementing good sleep practices directly prevents cardiovascular strain, enhances metabolic health, and lays the foundation for long-term physical well-being.
